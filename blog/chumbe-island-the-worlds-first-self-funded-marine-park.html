Chumbe Island, the World's First Self Funded Marine Park

Ocean Community·5 min read

Chumbe Island in Zanzibar is a marine park that pays for its own protection. No government budget keeps it running. The lodge itself pays for the park's rangers and reef monitoring, a model that has worked since the 1990s.

Negotiating a marine park from scratch

Sibylle Riedmiller began working in 1992 to set aside the coral reef and forest around Chumbe Island as a protected area. It took until 1994 for the government of Zanzibar to formally gazette it, making Chumbe Island Coral Park the first privately managed marine protected area anywhere in the world.

Funding conservation through the lodge itself

Most marine parks rely on government budgets or outside grants that can dry up. Chumbe took a different approach from the start. When the eco lodge opened in 1998, entrance fees and room rates became the funding source for rangers, reef monitoring and the park's operating costs, all in one. It was the first marine protected area anywhere to fund itself entirely this way.

Chumbe Island Coral Park became the first financially self sustaining marine protected area in the world when its eco lodge opened in 1998, six years after conservation work on the island began.

What the lodge actually funds

The lodge on Chumbe Island runs on solar power, composts its own waste and collects rainwater, keeping running costs low enough that ecotourism revenue alone can cover the park. Profits beyond that go toward marine education programs for schools on nearby Unguja Island, the main island of Zanzibar.
