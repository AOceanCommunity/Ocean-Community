Six Numbers: What These Conservation Ties Actually Fund

Ocean Community·6 min read

Every listing on this site carries a short conservation note, one or two lines by design. That brevity can make the claims feel abstract. So here are six of them pulled out and given room to actually mean something.

1,220 sq km, self funded

Misool Eco Resort in Raja Ampat, Indonesia doesn't donate to a reserve someone else runs. It funds and patrols its own, established in 2005, covering 1,220 square kilometers of reef. Fish biomass inside that area has risen 250 to 600% since the no take zones went in, which is the kind of number that only shows up when patrolling is real rather than nominal.

3 million coral larvae, one bad year

After the 2024 bleaching event, Six Senses Laamu's in house team, ten plus marine biologists working as the Maldives Underwater Initiative, released 3 million coral larvae onto damaged reef in the Laamu Atoll, working alongside Manta Trust, Blue Marine Foundation, and the Olive Ridley Project. That's disaster response, not brochure copy.

140 turtle nests, two seasons

Domes Aulus Zante sits on Laganas Bay inside the National Marine Park of Zakynthos, and adopted 70 loggerhead turtle nests from the beach in front of the resort in 2023, then another 70 in 2024, working with ARCHELON, Greece's sea turtle protection society. It also funded the rescue of an injured turtle. Two consecutive seasons of the same commitment is what separates a program from a press release.

These aren't cherry picked outliers. Every one of the 36 stays on Ocean Community has a note like this, and every one of them is checkable the same way: named organization, stated scope, a date attached. See how we vet a conservation claim for the process behind that.

A reserve declared by a chief, not a government

The waters in front of Likuliku Lagoon Resort on Malolo Island, Fiji were declared a marine protected area in 2005, not by national legislation but by the local paramount chief. Sea turtle sightings have increased since. It's a reminder that a lot of effective marine protection in the Pacific runs through customary authority, not a ministry.

Ranger patrols on a barrier reef

Turneffe Flats Lodge sits beside the Turneffe Atoll Marine Reserve, part of the Belize Barrier Reef, co managed by the Turneffe Atoll Sustainability Association. That organization runs the ranger patrols and fisheries enforcement across the reserve, the unglamorous, ongoing work that keeps a protected area protected between headlines.

A private island rebuilt from farmland

North Island Seychelles was once farmed for coconuts. Its Ark Project has spent years restoring the island to native rainforest and reintroducing giant tortoises, and it was among the first private islands to seek international marine protection status for its surrounding reefs. Green and hawksbill turtles are now returning to nest there, a slow, multi decade result rather than a single donation line.

Why the specificity matters

None of these numbers guarantee a property is perfect, and we don't claim otherwise. What they show is a difference in kind from "eco friendly" marketing copy: a name we can look up, a scope we can measure, and in most cases a timeline showing the work continuing year over year. That's the bar, applied the same way to all 36 stays.
