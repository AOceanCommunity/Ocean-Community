How We Vet a Conservation Claim

Ocean Community·5 min read

Type "eco friendly" into a hotel booking site and you'll get thousands of results. Almost none of them mean anything specific. We got tired of that word doing all the work while saying nothing, so before a property gets one of the 36 spots on this site, it has to survive a much duller question, can we actually check this.

Give it a name

The first thing we ask is whether the claim has a name attached to it. Not "supports sustainability initiatives" but a specific reserve, a specific program, a specific partner we can look up ourselves. Misool Eco Resort self funds and patrols its own 1,220 square kilometer marine reserve, established in 2005. That sentence has a name, a size and a date. We can go check whether it exists. A lot of what shows up in hotel marketing copy does not survive this question. That's fine. It just doesn't make the list.

Trace it back to something real

Once a claim has a name, we try to follow it somewhere outside the property's own website. The reserve's own materials, a partner nonprofit's page, news coverage, anything that isn't just the hotel talking about itself. Domes Aulus Zante's turtle nest adoptions are tied to ARCHELON, a named sea turtle protection group working the same bay. Lady Elliot Island's ban on plastic water bottles and its status as a climate change refuge from the Great Barrier Reef Foundation are both things we could verify without relying on the resort's own description of itself.

If we can't trace a claim back to something outside the property's own marketing, it doesn't make the cut. That's the whole bar and it applies the same way to all 36 stays on this site.

Ask for a number, not a feeling

"We care about the ocean" could describe almost any coastal hotel on the planet, which means it tells you nothing. What actually holds up is a number or a defined commitment, a donation per night, a percentage of profits, a research partnership with a stated scope. Gecko Hotel and Beach Club adds a discretionary one euro per night to guest bills, funding IbizaPreservation's seagrass work. That's specific enough to check now and specific enough to hold the property to later.

Check again later

A conservation tie that was true in 2021 is not automatically true today. Partnerships end, funding dries up, ownership changes hands. Part of why this list stays at 36 instead of growing fast is that we'd rather go back and recheck what's already here than keep adding new properties we haven't had time to look into properly.

What this actually means for you

Every listing links out to the property's own site to book. If a claim doesn't hold up under this process, the listing doesn't go up in the first place. That's really the whole system. It's less a formula than a habit of asking one boring question over and over, can we actually check this.
