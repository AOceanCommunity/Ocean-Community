Fiji's Chiefs Have Been Declaring Marine Reserves for Decades

Ocean Community·5 min read

Most marine reserves get declared by a national government. In Fiji, a huge share of the country's protected reef exists because a village chief said so, under a system of customary tenure that predates most of the country's environmental law.

Reefs that belong to villages, not the state

Under Fiji's qoliqoli system, customary fishing grounds are owned by the indigenous clans who live along that stretch of coast, not by the national government. That ownership comes with the power to open or close a reef to fishing, a power villages have used for generations, long before the word "marine protected area" existed.

The tabu, and how it works

A tabu is a no-take zone declared by a chief or a village council, sometimes temporary to let a depleted area recover, sometimes permanent. Break it and you are not just breaking a conservation rule, you are breaking a rule set by the people who will be fishing that reef for the rest of their lives. That is a different kind of enforcement than a sign and a distant park ranger.

The Fiji Locally Managed Marine Area Network, formed in 2000, grew within its first decade to cover roughly a quarter of Fiji's inshore fishing grounds across more than 200 qoliqoli, almost all of it built on tabu declared by individual villages.

Where NGOs and resorts fit in

Groups like the Pacific Blue Foundation do not run these reserves, villages do. What outside partners typically bring is money, coral propagation know how and monitoring data, layered on top of a management system the community already controls. A resort's role, when it is done honestly, is funding and reef labor rather than a claim to have started the whole thing.

Three stays on this list built around that system

Likuliku Lagoon Resort sits on waters the local paramount chief declared a marine protected area in 2005, with sea turtle sightings reported up since. Sau Bay Resort partners with the Pacific Blue Foundation on the Beqa Lagoon Initiative, planting more than 50 star shaped reef frames with coral colonies alongside ten local villages. Maqai Beach Eco Surf Resort runs coral gardening on a reef protected under a village agreed marine reserve, and has won Fiji's Excellence in Tourism sustainability award more than once.
