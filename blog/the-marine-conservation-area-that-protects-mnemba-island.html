The Marine Conservation Area That Protects Mnemba Island

Ocean Community·5 min read

Mnemba is barely a kilometer around, a sandbank of an island off the northeast tip of Unguja, Zanzibar's main island. The reef ringing it is why the whole thing is protected, and has been since 2002.

A tiny island, a large reef

Mnemba Island itself is small enough to walk around in under twenty minutes. What draws divers and a resident pod of bottlenose dolphins is the fringing reef around it, part of a wider coral system along Zanzibar's coast that is under increasing pressure from warming water and heavier tourism traffic across the archipelago.

Gazetted in 2002

The Mnemba Island Marine Conservation Area was formally gazetted by Zanzibar's government in 2002, setting rules on fishing methods, anchoring and how close boats can approach the dolphins that gather off the island's western side. Entry fees paid by visitors go toward funding that management, alongside the lodge operating on the island itself.

Mnemba Island Marine Conservation Area has been gazetted since 2002. Its current coral nursery and reef restoration research program runs from 2024 to 2027, run jointly with three universities.

The research program running now

Beyond the anchoring and fishing rules, the conservation area is currently the site of a funded coral nursery and reef restoration research program running from 2024 through 2027, a partnership between the conservation area's managers and three universities studying how the reef responds to restoration work over that multi year window.

Mnemba Island Lodge's place on this list

Mnemba Island Lodge sits inside the conservation area it is named for, run by andBeyond. It is tied directly to that 2024 to 2027 coral nursery and restoration research program, which is what put it on this list rather than a general sustainability claim.
