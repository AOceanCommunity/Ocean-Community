Brazil's Strictest Marine Protection Category Sits Off Santa Catarina

Ocean Community·5 min read

Not every protected area allows the same things. Brazil's conservation law splits reserves into two broad groups, areas built around sustainable use and areas built around leaving things alone entirely. The Arvoredo Marine Biological Reserve, off the coast of Santa Catarina, sits in the second group.

Two very different kinds of protection

Brazil's national system of protected areas, created by federal law in 2000, splits its reserve categories into two groups. Sustainable use areas allow some regulated extraction, controlled fishing or tourism infrastructure. Integral protection areas do not. A Reserva Biológica, a Biological Reserve, sits in the stricter group, built around scientific research and the smallest footprint of regulated visitation rather than any form of resource use.

Four islands and the water around them

Arvoredo Marine Biological Reserve covers a cluster of islands off the coast near Florianópolis and Bombinhas, protecting the rocky reef systems, kelp beds and open water between them. The reserve is known for consistent sightings of sea turtles, dolphins and rays, along with a density of tropical and subtropical reef fish unusual for this far south on Brazil's Atlantic coast.

A Biological Reserve sits in the strictest tier of Brazil's protected area system, built around research and minimal regulated access rather than any extractive use at all.

Visiting without disturbing it

Diving and snorkeling access to Arvoredo is tightly controlled compared to an average marine park, run through a limited number of licensed operators rather than open access. That restriction is the point. A reserve built to leave an ecosystem alone only works if visitation stays small enough not to become its own source of pressure.

Awasi Santa Catarina's place on this list

Awasi Santa Catarina, an all inclusive private villa lodge on Brazil's southern coast, sits a short boat ride from the reserve, giving guests access to a stretch of water that stays this intact specifically because so little is allowed to happen there.
