The License a Boat Needs to Watch Whales in the Azores

Ocean Community·4 min read

The Azores turned a whaling industry into a whale watching one decades ago. What keeps that second industry from repeating the mistakes of the first is a licensing system that decides which boats are allowed near a whale at all, and how.

A permit before a single trip leaves the harbor

Operating a whale watching boat in the Azores requires a license from the regional government, not just a boat and a captain willing to go looking. That license sets rules most tourists never see: how many boats can approach a single whale or pod at once, the minimum distance a boat has to keep, how long it can stay near the animal, and speed limits when a whale is nearby.

Why crowding is the real risk

A single boat watching a whale from a respectful distance is rarely the problem. The risk comes from multiple boats converging on the same animal at once, each operating independently, which can effectively trap a whale or force it to change its behavior just to get away from the attention. Licensing limits that by controlling how many commercial boats can be on the water pursuing whale watching at all, not just how each one behaves individually.

Licensed whale watching operators in the Azores work under rules governing approach distance, time spent near an animal, and the number of boats permitted near a single whale at once.

What this means for a guest booking a trip

Choosing a licensed operator is not just a formality. An unlicensed boat has no accountability to the rules that keep whale watching from becoming a new version of the pressure whales faced during the whaling era, just with cameras instead of harpoons. A hotel that specifically arranges trips through licensed operators is making that distinction on a guest's behalf before the booking even happens.

Santa Barbara Eco Beach Resort's place on this list

Santa Barbara Eco Beach Resort, a surf focused resort on São Miguel's north coast built with timber and cork, arranges whale watching for guests through the archipelago's licensed operator system rather than leaving it to chance.
