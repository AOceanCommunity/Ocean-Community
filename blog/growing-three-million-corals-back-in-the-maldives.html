Growing Three Million Corals Back in the Maldives

Ocean Community·5 min read

In 2024 a global bleaching event damaged reefs across the Maldives and around the world. At Laamu Atoll one resort's own marine biologists spent the following year growing coral back onto the reef, three million larvae at a time.

What a mass bleaching event actually does

Coral bleaching happens when ocean temperatures rise enough that coral expels the algae living inside it, the same algae that gives coral its color and much of its food. If temperatures stay high too long the coral starves and dies, turning what was a living reef into bare rock. The 2024 event was one of the largest bleaching events ever recorded, affecting reefs across the Pacific, Indian and Atlantic oceans at the same time.

A resort with its own marine research team

Six Senses Laamu runs the Maldives Underwater Initiative, a team of more than ten marine biologists based in a purpose built research center on the resort's own island. The team had already been running a coral larvae program since 2023, rearing larvae in tanks timed to the coral's natural lunar spawning cycle before moving them onto damaged sections of reef.

Three million larvae after the bleaching

When the 2024 bleaching event hit Laamu Atoll, the team responded by releasing three million coral larvae onto the reef in a single push, on top of the millions already released since the program began. They also used microfragmentation, a technique that cuts coral into small pieces to trigger faster regrowth, with fragments growing back twenty five to fifty times quicker than they would on their own.

Since 2023 the Maldives Underwater Initiative has released more than four million coral larvae onto damaged reef around Laamu Atoll, working alongside Manta Trust, Blue Marine Foundation and the Olive Ridley Project.
