How We Vet a Conservation Claim

Ocean Community·5 min read

"Eco friendly" shows up on almost every hotel website on earth. It means whatever the marketing team wants it to mean that week. We needed something with an actual bar to clear, so before any of the 36 stays on this site made the list, they had to pass the same test.

Named, not vague

The first filter is simple: can we name the thing. Not "supports sustainability initiatives," but a specific reserve, a specific program, a specific partner organization we can look up ourselves. Misool Eco Resort self funds and patrols its own 1,220 sq km marine reserve, established in 2005. That's a claim with a name, a size, and a date attached. We can check whether it exists. A lot of "eco" copy on hotel websites doesn't survive this first question.

Checkable, not just stated

Once a claim has a name, we try to trace it back to something real: the reserve's own materials, the partner nonprofit's site, news coverage, or the property's own detailed write up of the program. Domes Aulus Zante's turtle nest adoptions are tied to ARCHELON, a named sea turtle protection organization operating in the same bay. Lady Elliot Island's ban on plastic water bottles and its status as a Great Barrier Reef Foundation climate change refuge are things we could verify independently of the resort's own copy.

If we can't trace a claim back to something outside the property's own marketing, it doesn't make the cut. That's the whole bar, and it's the same bar for every one of the 36 stays on this site.

Specific, not general

"We care about the ocean" describes almost every coastal hotel on the planet and tells you nothing. What we look for instead is a number, a mechanism, or a stated commitment: a per night donation, a percentage of profits, a research partnership with a defined scope. Gecko Hotel and Beach Club adds a discretionary one euro per night to guest bills, funding IbizaPreservation's seagrass protection work. That's specific enough to check and specific enough to hold the property to later.

Rechecked, not set once

A conservation tie that was true in 2021 isn't automatically true now. Partnerships end, funding lapses, ownership changes. We're a small team, which is part of why this list stays at 36 stays instead of thousands: we'd rather revisit the properties we've already vetted than keep adding new ones we haven't had time to check properly.

What this means for you

Every listing on Ocean Community links out to the property's own site for booking. We don't process bookings and we don't take a commission, so there's no financial reason for us to loosen this bar to fit more properties in. If a claim doesn't hold up, the listing doesn't go up either.
