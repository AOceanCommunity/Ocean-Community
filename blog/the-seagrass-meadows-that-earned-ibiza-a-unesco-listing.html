The Seagrass Meadows That Earned Ibiza a UNESCO Listing

Ocean Community·4 min read

Ibiza's UNESCO World Heritage listing gets credited to the old town and the nightlife reputation that came later. The actual citation, from 1999, leans just as heavily on something underwater: one of the best preserved stretches of Posidonia oceanica in the Mediterranean.

A seagrass, not a weed

Posidonia oceanica is a flowering plant, not an algae and it grows nowhere outside the Mediterranean. It forms dense underwater meadows in shallow coastal water, the same meadows that give the sea around Ibiza and Formentera its unusually clear, almost swimming pool blue color. The plant filters sediment out of the water column and anchors the seafloor with roots, which is most of why the water stays that clear in the first place.

Slow enough to outlive empires

Posidonia grows by extending its rhizome, the underground stem, by roughly one to a few centimeters a year. Some of the connected meadows in the Mediterranean are estimated to be thousands of years old, among the oldest living organisms documented anywhere. That slowness cuts both ways: a meadow can take centuries to recover from a single anchor drag or a trawled seabed.

UNESCO's 1999 listing for Ibiza cites its Posidonia meadows by name, calling them home to an exceptional biodiversity including several endangered marine species.

What actually threatens it

Anchoring is the most immediate damage, a single boat dragging its chain across a meadow can tear out growth that took decades to form. Coastal development, pollution runoff and warming water all add pressure on top of that. Posidonia has been retreating across parts of the Mediterranean for years, which is part of why a scatter of small, specific funding efforts around Ibiza and Formentera matters more than it might look.

Two stays on this list funding that work

Gecko Hotel and Beach Club, on Formentera, adds a discretionary one euro per night to guest bills that goes to IbizaPreservation's Posidonia protection work. Sir Joan, in Ibiza town, runs the same one euro per night model, funding IbizaPreservation's wider land and sea conservation projects around the islands.
