Belize's Largest Atoll Took Until 2012 to Become a Reserve

Ocean Community·5 min read

Turneffe Atoll is the largest atoll in the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the second largest barrier reef system in the world. It took until 2012 for Belize to actually declare it a marine reserve.

The largest atoll in the region

Turneffe Atoll sits off the coast of Belize as part of the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, a reef system that runs along the coastlines of Mexico, Belize, Guatemala and Honduras and ranks as the second largest barrier reef in the world after Australia's. Turneffe itself is the largest atoll anywhere in that system, made up of more than 150 mangrove islands surrounding productive flats, creeks and lagoons.

Declared a reserve in 2012

Belize formally declared Turneffe Atoll a marine reserve on November 22, 2012, making it the largest marine reserve in the country at roughly 131,690 hectares. The reserve protects more than 500 fish species and 65 species of stony coral, along with sea turtles, manatees and dolphins that live in its waters.

Turneffe Atoll became a marine reserve in 2012 and Belize's largest one, protecting more than 500 fish species across 131,690 hectares of reef, mangrove and open water.

Who actually runs it day to day

A government designation on its own does not patrol a reef or enforce fishing rules. The following year, in October 2013, Belize's Fisheries Department appointed the Turneffe Atoll Sustainability Association to co manage the reserve's daily operations, from ranger patrols to fisheries enforcement across the atoll.

Turneffe Flats Lodge's place on this list

Turneffe Flats Lodge sits on the eastern edge of the atoll, right beside the reserve itself. The lodge sits beside the Turneffe Atoll Marine Reserve, co managed by the Turneffe Atoll Sustainability Association, which runs ranger patrols and fisheries enforcement across the reserve.
