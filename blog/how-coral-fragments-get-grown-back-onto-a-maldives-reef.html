How Coral Fragments Get Grown Back Onto a Maldives Reef

Ocean Community·4 min read

Not every coral restoration project starts with a larva. Coral fragmentation builds a reef back the way a gardener propagates a plant from a cutting and one Maldives resort has over 3,500 fragments growing through that process at once.

Coral can regrow from a piece of itself

Coral is a colony of thousands of small animals, polyps, sharing one connected skeleton. Because of that structure, a broken piece of healthy coral can often reattach and keep growing rather than dying, the same basic principle behind taking a cutting from a plant. Restoration teams use this directly: a small fragment, sometimes no larger than a few centimeters, can become the seed for an entire new colony.

Nurseries before the reef

Fragments are rarely placed straight back onto open reef. Most programs grow them first on a nursery structure, a frame, rope line or table anchored in calmer water, where the coral can heal and grow in a more controlled setting before it faces the currents and competition of the open reef. Once a fragment has grown large enough to have a reasonable survival chance, it gets transplanted onto degraded reef that needs the coverage.

Coral fragmentation builds new colonies from pieces of existing coral rather than from larvae, letting a single healthy colony seed dozens of new growth points across a damaged reef.

Why this differs from larval restoration

Growing coral from larvae, the approach behind large scale projects elsewhere in the Maldives, can seed far more individual colonies at once but depends on successful spawning events and survives at a much lower rate per larva. Fragmentation works with fewer starting pieces but a far higher survival rate per fragment, since each one is already a functioning, mature piece of coral rather than a larva that has to establish itself from nothing.

Conrad Maldives Rangali Island's place on this list

Conrad Maldives Rangali Island, a twin island overwater resort certified as a PADI Five Star Dive Resort and Eco Center, nurtures over 3,500 coral fragments in an ongoing reef restoration initiative built around exactly this method.
