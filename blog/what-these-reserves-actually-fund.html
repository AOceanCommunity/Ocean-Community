Six Numbers Behind the Conservation Ties on This List

Ocean Community·6 min read

Every listing on this site gets one or two lines about its conservation tie, because that's what fits. But a line like that can start to feel abstract if you read enough of them in a row. So here are six, pulled out of their boxes and given some room.

1,220 square kilometers, paid for by the resort itself

Misool Eco Resort in Raja Ampat doesn't donate to somebody else's reserve. It funds and patrols its own, established in 2005, covering 1,220 square kilometers of reef. Fish biomass inside that area has risen 250 to 600 percent since the no take zones went in. That kind of number only shows up when the patrolling is real rather than a line item on a brochure.

3 million coral larvae in one bad year

After the 2024 bleaching event, the in house team at Six Senses Laamu, over ten marine biologists working as the Maldives Underwater Initiative, released 3 million coral larvae onto damaged reef in the Laamu Atoll, alongside Manta Trust, Blue Marine Foundation and the Olive Ridley Project. That's disaster response, not brochure copy.

140 turtle nests over two seasons

Domes Aulus Zante sits on Laganas Bay inside the National Marine Park of Zakynthos. It adopted 70 loggerhead turtle nests from the beach out front in 2023, then another 70 in 2024, working with ARCHELON, Greece's sea turtle protection society. It also funded the rescue of an injured turtle along the way. Doing it twice in a row is what separates a program from a headline.

None of these are cherry picked outliers. Every one of the 36 stays here has a note like this and every one is checkable the same way, a named organization with a stated scope and a date attached. See how we vet a conservation claim for the process behind that.

A reserve declared by a chief, not a ministry

The waters in front of Likuliku Lagoon Resort on Malolo Island, Fiji were declared a marine protected area in 2005, not through national legislation but by the local paramount chief. Sea turtle sightings have gone up since. It's a good reminder that a lot of effective marine protection in the Pacific runs through customary authority rather than a government office.

Ranger patrols on a barrier reef

Turneffe Flats Lodge sits beside the Turneffe Atoll Marine Reserve, part of the Belize Barrier Reef, co managed by the Turneffe Atoll Sustainability Association. That group runs the ranger patrols and fisheries enforcement across the reserve, the unglamorous work that keeps a protected area protected between headlines.

A farm turned back into an island

North Island Seychelles was once farmed for coconuts. Its Ark Project has spent years restoring the island to native rainforest and reintroducing giant tortoises. It was also among the first private islands to seek international marine protection status for the reefs around it. Green and hawksbill turtles are now nesting there again, which is the kind of result that takes decades rather than a single donation.

Why we bother with the specifics

None of this guarantees a property is perfect and we're not claiming it does. What it shows is the difference between marketing language and something you can actually verify, a name you can look up, a scope you can measure and usually a timeline showing the work has kept going year after year. That's the bar, applied the same way across all 36 stays.
