The Fishing Village That Became Brazil's Sea Turtle Headquarters

Ocean Community·5 min read

In 1977 a group of oceanography students watched fishermen kill eleven sea turtles in a single afternoon at an atoll off Brazil's coast. That moment led to a national program that now runs twenty one bases along more than 1,100 kilometers of coastline, headquartered in a village called Praia do Forte.

The expedition that started it

In 1977 students from the Faculty of Oceanography of Rio Grande took part in an expedition to Rocas Atoll in northern Brazil. What they saw there, fishermen killing eleven sea turtles at once, became the reason a small group of researchers started what would grow into Brazil's national sea turtle program a few years later.

From a research project to a national program

The program that became known as TAMAR, short for tartarugas marinhas, sea turtles in Portuguese, started work in 1980. A support foundation followed in 1988 to help fund research, run education programs and work with the fishing communities whose daily lives overlapped with turtle nesting grounds.

TAMAR now runs twenty one bases along more than 1,100 kilometers of Brazilian coastline, protecting sea turtles along with sharks, rays and coral in the waters around them.

Why Praia do Forte became its headquarters

Praia do Forte, a small village on Bahia's coast, was chosen as the program's national headquarters because it sits on one of the most important sea turtle nesting beaches in Brazil. The visitor center built there is now the largest of the program's bases and the one most visitors to the region actually see.

Tivoli Ecoresort Praia do Forte's place on this list

Tivoli Ecoresort Praia do Forte sits right beside the village where TAMAR began, on what locals call Brazil's Whale Coast.
